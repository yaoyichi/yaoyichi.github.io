Why Data Science?

Data Science turns AI capability into evidence and decisions.

You may arrive with a field already in mind—or with several interests you want to explore. Data Science gives you the statistical, computational, and data-system tools to frame useful AI questions, prepare evidence, develop and evaluate methods, and translate results into decisions people can use.

Come with a defined field, several possibilities, or a desire to explore. Preparation in calculus, linear algebra, statistics, and programming equips you to begin.

Choose Data Science when you want to connect the whole system.

Questions, real-world data, statistical evidence, modeling, computation, and decisions belong together. AI and machine learning are part of the toolkit; Data Science teaches you how the problem, data, method, evidence, and decision fit together.

Your role in an AI-enabled world

Understanding data is how AI creates real-world impact.

Understanding how data is created, represented, and interpreted helps you see what an AI system can learn, what it may miss, and how much confidence its output deserves. Data Science prepares you to frame the problem, evaluate the evidence, and decide how AI should be used in real work.

  1. 01 Frame the problem

    Turn a real-world goal or curiosity into a question that data—and AI—can help answer.

  2. 02 Build trustworthy data

    Collect, prepare, represent, and interrogate the real-world data that AI methods depend on.

  3. 03 Learn from data

    Combine statistics, algorithms, machine learning, and AI to identify patterns, test ideas, and measure uncertainty.

  4. 04 Put evidence to work

    Implement Data Science and AI methods at scale, communicate their meaning, and apply the full process in advanced coursework or a Plan B capstone.

How a Plan B capstone works

Move from a real question to evidence you can explain.

Plan B is the faculty-guided process behind the examples above. Students develop a question, work with data, choose and evaluate methods, and communicate the result through a written report, oral presentation, and poster.

  1. 01Define the question

    Clarify the decision, phenomenon, or system you want to understand.

  2. 02Build the evidence

    Find, prepare, represent, and examine the data the question requires.

  3. 03Develop and evaluate

    Apply statistical, computational, machine-learning, or AI methods and test what they show.

  4. 04Explain the result

    Present the work, its uncertainty, and what someone can responsibly do with the evidence.

Every project is different. Advisor availability, project scope, data access, and sharing permissions must be confirmed.

Do you have the foundation to begin?

Applicants arrive from many academic backgrounds and with different levels of certainty about where they want to apply Data Science and AI. This is a quantitative graduate program, so successful applicants should arrive prepared for advanced work in mathematics, statistics, and computing.

How many credits is the University of Minnesota Data Science M.S.?

The program requires 31 graduate credits. Both Plan B and Plan C require at least 25 credits of regular coursework; up to 6 credits may come from directed research or, for Plan B, a combination of directed research and capstone work.

How do Plan B and Plan C work?

Every admitted student starts in Plan C, the coursework-only path. A student can switch to Plan B after an eligible faculty member agrees to advise the capstone. Plan B projects commonly take one or two semesters.

How long does the Data Science M.S. take?

The recommended pace is two to three regular courses in fall and spring, supporting completion in 18 to 24 months. Advanced students may use an accelerated fall, spring, and summer schedule to finish in 12 months. Part-time study can take longer.

Can I apply with one or two prerequisites in progress?

Yes. Missing one or two prerequisites does not automatically exclude an otherwise strong applicant. Explain what remains and how you plan to complete it; conditional admission may be possible.

How much programming preparation should I have?

Plan for preparation equivalent to two introductory computer science courses using a general-purpose language such as Python, Java, C, or C++, including algorithms and data structures. R or MATLAB is useful additional preparation but is not a substitute.

Is the GRE required?

No. The program does not require or accept GRE scores, and submitted GRE scores are not reviewed.

Can I complete the degree fully online?

Do not plan on a fully online degree. Some requirements may be available through UNITE, but the program does not guarantee that every requirement can be completed remotely, and some courses or capstone work may require campus visits.

Is the Data Science M.S. a STEM program?

Yes. The University lists the Data Science M.S. as a STEM program with CIP code 11.0401.

Can graduate credits transfer?

Transfer credit may be possible under University policy. At least 20 degree credits must be completed at the University of Minnesota while enrolled as an admitted Data Science M.S. student.

What funding should I plan for?

Students should plan to provide their own funding. Teaching or research assistantships for M.S. students are uncommon and are not guaranteed; current tuition rates are maintained by One Stop.

Where can I ask a question about my preparation?

Email the graduate admissions team at csadmit@umn.edu. Include the prerequisite area and enough detail for the team to understand your question.
